Within Reach

Public transit routes and access to jobs and essential services

Sierra Arnold, Xavier University

Households without a car are largely limited to the jobs and services they can reach by public transit. This interactive mapping tool shows the public transit where you live, and how many jobs and which services the residents of each neighborhood can reach.

  • The Routes and buses tab shows each metro's public transit routes, with its buses and trains moving on their morning schedules over where the jobs are.
  • The Jobs and essential services tab counts the jobs, grocery stores, banks, hospitals, libraries and community colleges within reach of each neighborhood (a Census block group), by public transit and by car.

About the data

Each block group is colored by how many jobs, or facilities of one essential service, its residents can reach within the chosen travel time, by public transit or by car, or by how many times more a car reaches. Darker orange means fewer and darker blue more; on the car vs. public transit map, orange marks the largest car advantage. By default the colors are the same at every travel time, so the map changes as the slider moves. The color scale menu in the legend can instead fit them to the chosen travel time or, for jobs, to what is on screen.

Trips

By public transit: door-to-door trips by walking and public transit, including waits and transfers, timed as the median over departures between 7 and 9 a.m. on one weekday per quarter, on the schedules archived for that day. Public transit times are from 2023 to 2025, each metro's latest year of usable schedules. By car: driving with no traffic, so the car counts are an upper bound. Driving times are from 2026 street maps.

What is counted

  • Jobs: all jobs in the Census Bureau's LODES, 2023 (2016 in Alaska and 2021 in Michigan, the latest years published there).
  • Grocery stores: supermarkets, superstores and large grocery stores that accept SNAP (USDA).
  • Banks: full-service bank offices (FDIC).
  • Hospitals: general acute care hospitals (CMS).
  • Libraries: central and branch public libraries (IMLS).
  • Community colleges: public two-year college campuses (NCES).

Neighborhood values

Population, median household income, the share of people below twice the poverty line, the share of households without a car and the shares by race and ethnicity come from the American Community Survey, 2018–22. White, Black and Asian count people who are not Hispanic; Hispanic counts people of any race. The income filter splits each metro's block groups into four groups of equal size by median household income. The race and ethnicity filter keeps majority-White block groups, where more than half of the residents are White and not Hispanic, or majority non-White ones, where more than half are Black, Asian, Hispanic (of any race) or of another group. For Hartford, Jacksonville, Palm Bay and Riverside, which the papers' data lack, the census values come from the same survey files, added for this map.

Routes and buses

The Routes and buses tab draws each metro's public transit routes and moves its buses and trains on one weekday's published schedules, from 6:30 to 9:30 a.m., over the jobs per square mile of each block group. Its own About window lists each metro's schedules and date.

Glossary

Block group
A Census Bureau area of about 600 to 3,000 people, the smallest the map shows; the map also calls it a neighborhood.
Median block group
The middle block group of a metro: half reach more and half reach less. The metro overview uses it to describe the metro.
Higher than X%
For a block group, the share of its metro's block groups with a lower value; in the metro overview, the share of the mapped metros with a lower median.
