Within Reach

Access to jobs and essential services by public transit and car

Sierra Arnold, Xavier University

This map counts what the residents of a neighborhood (a Census block group) can reach by public transit and by car: jobs, grocery stores, banks, hospitals, libraries and community colleges. Choose a travel time from 15 minutes to an hour. It covers 84 U.S. metropolitan areas.

About the data

Each block group is colored by how many jobs, or facilities of one essential service, its residents can reach within the chosen travel time, by public transit or by car, or by how many times more a car reaches. Orange is below the median block group and blue is above it; on the car vs. public transit map, orange marks the largest car advantage.

Trips

By public transit: door-to-door trips by walking and public transit, including waits and transfers, timed as the median over departures between 7 and 9 a.m. on one weekday per quarter, on the schedules archived for that day. Each metro shows its latest year of usable schedules. By car: driving with no traffic, so the car counts are an upper bound.

What is counted

  • Jobs: all jobs in the Census Bureau's LODES, 2023 (2016 in Alaska and 2021 in Michigan, the latest years published there).
  • Grocery stores: supermarkets, super stores and large grocery stores that accept SNAP (USDA).
  • Banks: full-service bank offices (FDIC).
  • Hospitals: general acute care hospitals (CMS).
  • Libraries: central and branch public libraries (IMLS).
  • Community colleges: public two-year college campuses (NCES).

Neighborhood values

Population, median household income and the share of households without a car come from the American Community Survey, 2018–22. The income filter splits each metro's block groups into four equal groups by income. The data have no census values for Hartford, Jacksonville, Palm Bay or Riverside.
