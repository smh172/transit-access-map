Access by transit and car

Block groups in 84 U.S. metropolitan areas, 2023–2025

How many jobs can the residents of a neighborhood reach by public transit, and how many grocery stores, banks, hospitals, libraries and community colleges? How many times more could they reach by car? This map shows both for every block group, a neighborhood of about 600 to 3,000 people, in 84 metropolitan areas.

  • Choose what to count at the top of the panel: jobs within 30 minutes, or an essential service within 45 minutes.
  • Hover over a neighborhood to see its numbers and where it stands in its metro. Click to keep it in the panel.
  • Choose a view: by transit, by car, or how many times more a car reaches.
  • Filter neighborhoods by income and by the share of households without a car.

About the data

Each block group is colored by the number of jobs its residents can reach within 30 minutes, or of facilities of one essential service within 45 minutes, by transit or by car, or by how many times more a car reaches.

Transit

Door-to-door trips by walking and transit, including the walk, the wait, the ride and any transfer. The time is the median over departures between 7:00 and 9:00 a.m. on one weekday per quarter, routed on the transit schedules archived for that day. The value is the average over the usable quarters of the metro's latest year with usable schedules, which is 2025 for most metros (see Coverage).

Car

Driving times on the street network with no traffic. The car counts are an upper bound, since real trips at rush hour are slower.

Jobs

All jobs in the Census Bureau's LODES, counted at their workplaces, in 2023, the latest year available when the data were built. The transit values for 2024 and 2025 also count 2023's jobs, so both modes count the same jobs. LODES has no data after 2016 for Alaska or after 2021 for Michigan, so the metros in those states count jobs from those years.

Essential services

Each service counts the facilities reachable within 45 minutes, the threshold of the essential-services paper, with that paper's definitions: grocery stores are supermarkets, super stores and large grocery stores authorized to accept SNAP (USDA); banks are full-service bank offices (FDIC Summary of Deposits); hospitals are general acute care hospitals (CMS Provider of Services); libraries are central and branch public libraries (IMLS Public Libraries Survey); community colleges are public two-year college campuses (NCES IPEDS). Transit and car counts use the facilities of the metro's year, except that 2025 uses 2024's libraries and community colleges. The nearest-facility time is the transit trip to the closest one; trips longer than 90 minutes count as none. Because many block groups reach none of a service by transit, its colors use fixed groups, with none as the first.

Filters

The filters use the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, 2018–22 five-year estimates: median household income (table B19013) and the share of households with no vehicle available (table B25044). The income filter splits each metro's block groups into four equal groups by median household income. The data have no census values for Hartford, Jacksonville, Palm Bay or Riverside, and the survey does not report an income for some small block groups; the filters leave those block groups out.

Colors

Each color holds about a tenth of the block groups. Red is below the median block group and teal is above it. With "This view", the tenths are recomputed for the block groups on screen. On the car vs. transit map the colors run the other way, so red marks the largest car advantage.
